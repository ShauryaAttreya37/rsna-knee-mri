Knee MRI Abnormality Detection

by Shaurya Attreya · @ShauryaAttreya37

A weakly supervised, multi-series deep learning pipeline that predicts twelve knee abnormalities from raw MRI DICOM studies, built for the 2026 RSNA Knee Abnormality Detection challenge on Kaggle. Every module, test, and experiment in this project was designed and written by me.

The problem

Each study is a full knee MRI session: several series (sagittal, coronal, axial; fluid-sensitive or not; fat-suppressed or not), each made of dozens of DICOM slices from scanners all over the world. The task is to output a probability for each of twelve findings: ACL and MCL injury, medial and lateral meniscal tears, medial, lateral, and patellofemoral osteoarthritis, effusion, synovitis, Baker's cyst, bone contusion, and fracture.

The hard part is supervision. Only 58 of 4,407 training studies have expert labels. Every study has its original radiology report, often not in English, but reports are not available at test time, so the final model must work from images alone.

Approach

  1. Audit before modelling. A read-only, resumable inventory of every DICOM header and an exhaustive pixel-decode pass produced explicit exclusion policies for corrupt series, plus geometry audits of slice orientation, pixel spacing, and physical field of view. (dicom_audit.py, physical_geometry_audit.py)
  2. Turn reports into labels. A local Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct extractor read multilingual reports and emitted per-finding probabilities for the 4,349 unlabeled studies. Benchmarked against the 58 gold studies, it reached macro AUC 0.753. An equal-mean ensemble of three public CC0 label sets reached 0.896 and became the weak target. (report_extractor.py, weak_label_benchmark.py)
  3. Leakage-safe validation. Deterministic multilabel-stratified five-fold splits over the gold studies. Gold studies are removed from weak pretraining entirely, so no validation image is ever seen in training. (gold_folds.py)
  4. Model. A DINOv2 ViT-S/14 encoder embeds each slice. Target-specific attention pools slices into a series vector, series metadata is appended, and a second target-specific attention pools series into a study vector. Weak pretraining on 4,349 studies is followed by gold fine-tuning per fold. (imaging_training.py)
  5. Offline inference. Each test study is decoded once, every fold checkpoint scores it, and scores are rank-averaged per target to cancel calibration drift between checkpoints. The submission is validated for exact columns, identifier order, and finite in-range probabilities before it is written. (imaging_inference.py, submission.py)

Preprocessing: why the full field of view stays primary

Scanners differ widely. Across 24,386 series, the shorter in-plane field of view spans 70 to 310 mm, with the middle 90% between 140 and 182 mm, and pixel spacing runs from 0.17 to 0.56 mm over the same percentiles. A fixed crop would cut anatomy from some series and pad others, so every series is fit into 224 × 224 by physical size with symmetric padding. A square crop is only an optional detail view, and the audit picked its size: the largest candidate that needs no padding for at least 90% of series, overall and in every plane.

Weak labels: which source to trust

Scoring each label source against the expert labels came before any imaging change. The ensemble beat the Qwen extractor on all twelve findings, with macro AUC 0.896 against 0.753. The ensemble covers 57 of the 58 gold studies; Qwen covers all 58. This comparison chose a label source. It does not estimate leaderboard performance.

Code walkthrough

Two excerpts from the repository, unmodified apart from trimmed input checks. Everything else is one click away on GitHub.

Target-specific attention over slices and series

Each finding gets its own attention weights, so the ACL head can focus on sagittal slices through the notch while the effusion head looks at the suprapatellar recess. Series metadata (plane, fluid sensitivity, fat suppression) is fused before series-level pooling.

Rank-averaged fold ensemble

Pooled out-of-fold analysis showed that fold checkpoints rank studies well but disagree on score scale. Averaging percentile ranks per target instead of raw probabilities removes that drift at test time without using labels.
